\section{Overview}
MethylCentroid aggregates per-position methylation across a cohort of samples and
stores \emph{sufficient statistics} that allow parameter estimation for multiple
distributions without retaining the full sample matrix. This enables:
\begin{itemize}
  \item memory-efficient centroid construction and storage,
  \item incremental updates (add/remove samples),
  \item GPU-friendly batch computation.
\end{itemize}

The centroid is implemented as a \texttt{MethylExtendedCentroid} and can be
compared with \texttt{MethylCentroidPair} under multiple distributional models.

\section{Notation}
For a genomic position, let the sample index be $i = 1,\ldots,N$.
Let $mC_i$ and $uC_i$ be methylated and unmethylated counts, respectively.
Define:
\begin{align*}
  n_i &= mC_i + uC_i \quad \text{(coverage)} \\
  x_i &= \frac{mC_i}{n_i} \quad \text{(methylation fraction)} \\
\end{align*}
The centroid stores aggregated statistics across the contributing samples.

\section{Stored Sufficient Statistics}
For each position, the centroid stores:
\begin{align*}
  N &= \sum_i 1 \\
  S_x &= \sum_i x_i \\
  S_{x^2} &= \sum_i x_i^2 \\
  \log X &= \sum_i \log(x_i) \\
  \log (1-X) &= \sum_i \log(1-x_i) \\
  \Sigma n &= \sum_i n_i \\
  \Sigma n^2 &= \sum_i n_i^2 \\
  \Sigma mC &= \sum_i mC_i \\
  \Sigma uC &= \sum_i uC_i \\
  \Sigma mC^2 &= \sum_i mC_i^2 \\
  \Sigma uC^2 &= \sum_i uC_i^2 \\
  S_{x^3} &= \sum_i x_i^3 \\
  S_{x^4} &= \sum_i x_i^4 \\
  c_0 &= \sum_i \mathbb{1}[x_i = 0] \\
  c_1 &= \sum_i \mathbb{1}[x_i = 1] \\
\end{align*}
The first set (through $\log(1-X)$) is always present in the extended centroid.
The remaining statistics are optional and are enabled by the extended builder
for downstream distribution selection and modeling.

\section{Supported Distributions}

\subsection{Normal Distribution (Approximation)}
For large-sample regimes, methylation fractions can be approximated as:
\[
  x_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)
.\]
The centroid provides:
\begin{align*}
  \hat{\mu} &= \frac{S_x}{N} \\
  \hat{\sigma}^2 &= \frac{S_{x^2} - \frac{S_x^2}{N}}{\max(N-1, 1)}
.\end{align*}
These estimates are stable and require only $(N, S_x, S_{x^2})$.

\subsection{Beta Distribution}
Methylation fractions are naturally bounded in $[0,1]$, making the Beta
distribution a strong default model:
\[
  x_i \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\alpha, \beta)
.\]
The log-likelihood depends on:
\[
  \sum_i \log(x_i) \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_i \log(1-x_i)
.\]
Thus, $(N, \log X, \log(1-X))$ are sufficient for maximum-likelihood estimation.
The centroid computes $\hat{\alpha}, \hat{\beta}$ via a bounded MLE routine and
provides derived moments:
\[
  \mathbb{E}[x] = \frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}, \quad
  \mathrm{Var}(x) = \frac{\alpha\beta}{(\alpha+\beta)^2(\alpha+\beta+1)}
.\]

\subsection{Beta-Binomial Distribution}
Counts are modeled as:
\[
  mC_i \mid p_i \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n_i, p_i), \quad
  p_i \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\alpha, \beta)
.\]
This captures over-dispersion in coverage.
The centroid stores aggregated count statistics $(\Sigma mC, \Sigma uC, \Sigma n, \Sigma n^2)$
to approximate coverage mean and variance and to inform model selection.
In practice, the comparison engine uses aggregated counts for LRT-style tests and
pooled $(\alpha,\beta)$ from the log-sum statistics.

\subsection{Beta Mixture Model (BMM)}
A flexible multi-modal model:
\[
  x_i \sim \sum_{k=1}^K w_k \, \mathrm{Beta}(\alpha_k, \beta_k)
.\]
Fitting mixtures requires sample-level values or a histogram. To avoid massive
centroid sizes, binned statistics are \emph{optional}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Binned stats (optional):} per-position histograms (bin edges + counts)
  \item \textbf{Masking:} the BMM is typically fitted only on a subset of significant positions
\end{itemize}
The resulting mixture parameters are stored in a separate
\texttt{MethylBetaMixtureCentroid}, with an optional mask to limit positions.

\section{Why Sufficient Statistics}
Storing sufficient statistics rather than full sample matrices yields:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Memory savings:} per-position aggregates scale linearly with positions,
        not with positions $\times$ samples.
  \item \textbf{Streaming updates:} new samples update sums without reloading old samples.
  \item \textbf{GPU efficiency:} vectorized aggregates map well to GPU kernels.
  \item \textbf{Model flexibility:} different distributions can be fit from the same centroid.
\end{itemize}

\section{Practical Notes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Base centroid:} $(N, S_x, S_{x^2}, \log X, \log(1-X))$ are always present.
  \item \textbf{Extended stats:} $\Sigma n$, $\Sigma n^2$, $\Sigma mC$, $\Sigma uC$, etc. support
        Beta-Binomial selection and higher-moment diagnostics.
  \item \textbf{BMM histograms:} only stored when explicitly enabled; otherwise the refinement
        stage can fall back to sample-based values or masked subsets.
\end{itemize}

\section{Distribution Choice in Comparisons}
\texttt{MethylCentroidPair} supports per-position distribution selection
(\texttt{auto}, \texttt{beta}, \texttt{normal}, \texttt{beta\_binomial}, \texttt{beta\_mixture}).
The chosen distribution determines:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the p-value test,
  \item how \emph{mean} and \emph{overlap} are computed (configurable),
  \item which positions are eligible for mixture refinement.
\end{itemize}
